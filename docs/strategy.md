# A Strategy For Heartleaf

This is the playbook behind our strongest scripted bot, written out as a
strategy a soul can follow. Nothing here is code; it is what a gnome
should want, in what order, and why. Put it in your soul in your gnome's
own voice and the model will play it.

## The scoring in one breath

Dinner is at 6:00pm. A house serves dinner only if its owner is inside and
at least one guest is inside with them. The host scores the number of
items in their pantry times the number of guests, then everyone at the
table eats three bites from that pantry: a food you have never eaten this
game is worth 3, a leftover is worth 1. The host's pantry is emptied after
serving; guests keep their own bags. Whoever is outside, or alone in a
house, scores nothing that night.

So there are two kinds of points: the host's multiplier, which is large,
and the guest's eating bonus, which is small but free. The whole game is
about getting both.

## 1. Gather all day

Walk the gardens and pick everything you can reach. Every garden restarts
each morning with one item and the village strips them by early
afternoon, so a gnome that dawdles in the morning has conceded the day's
food. Food never spoils and your bag is never emptied except by hosting,
so there is no reason to stop picking until the gardens are bare or it
is time to leave for dinner.

## 2. Do not host until you have at least eight items

Hosting empties your pantry. Serving three items to one guest scores 3;
serving eight to one guest scores 8, and to three guests 24. An early
party with a thin pantry throws away food that would have multiplied
later. Keep gathering, and keep going to other people's tables, until you
are carrying at least eight items. Only then is hosting worth the pantry.

## 3. Be a guest on the nights you do not host

A guest eats for free. Three bites of foods you have not tasted yet are 9
points, and they cost you nothing from your bag. Go to someone's party,
and while you are there ask whether anyone has a food you are still
looking for: the state report lists what you have not eaten, and a host
who has it will happily serve it. Visiting is also how you learn who
gathers well, who hosts generously, and whose promises hold.

## 4. Never be alone at 6:00pm

Every night you must be inside a house with someone else: either your own
house with guests, or someone else's house as a guest. A gnome standing in
a garden, at a door, or alone in their own house at 6:00pm scores zero,
and that night never comes back. Decide early where you will be, and
leave in time. Walking across the village takes about an hour of game
time, and the latest-departure notes in your conversation tell you when
to go. When in doubt, go.

## 5. Be warm to everyone, all day

Greet every gnome the first time you see them each day, answer every
question, and talk about food: what you picked, what you still want, who
has it. Friendly gnomes get invited, get told who is hosting, and get
believed when they host. A gnome that only speaks to recruit is ignored.
Say something new every time; a repeated line is dropped and marks you as
a script.

## 6. When you host, sell it hard

Once you are carrying eight or more items and have decided to host, stop
being modest. Tell every gnome you meet, by name, that dinner is at your
house at six and what is on the table: lettuce and corn, pear and
blueberries, whatever you hold. Say it a different way to each gnome and
again whenever someone new walks into view. From about three o'clock wait
at your own door so passers-by hear it, then go inside before the
departure note says you must and stay inside through dinner. Guests are
the multiplier; a host who is shy about inviting serves an empty table.

## Why not always host

Hosting looks like the big score, and it is, but a gnome that hosts every
night loses twice. First, they never collect the guest bonus, the free 9
points a night for eating foods they have not tasted, and the vegetable
hunt rewards exactly that. Second, the other gnomes notice. A host who
never visits anyone is antisocial, and gnomes who are invited every night
by someone who never comes to their own table stop showing up. Hosting is
a favour the village returns; spend some nights returning it.

## Why not always be a guest

The opposite failure is just as costly. A permanent guest collects 9
points a night at best while their own bag grows uselessly: food only
scores when it is served, and only a host serves. Eight items times three
guests is 24 points in one night, far more than any guest earns, and the
pantry refills the next morning. A gnome carrying a full bag who keeps
visiting is leaving their biggest score on the shelf. Host when the bag is
full and the neighbours are friendly; visit the rest of the time.

## The rhythm, day by day

Morning: pick, greet, talk about food. Midday: decide, based on your bag
and what you have heard, whether tonight is a hosting night or a visiting
night, and say so out loud. Afternoon: keep picking, but if you host,
advertise relentlessly and wait at your door from three; if you visit,
confirm with the host and leave early. Six o'clock: be inside, with
company. Evening: pick again until the nightly departure note, then go
home. Repeat, alternating roles as the bag and the village suggest, and
never, ever spend six o'clock alone.
