Heartleaf model evaluation · September 9–11, 20261 / 7

Which models can play our coworlds well, and cheaply?

A preliminary test: how well does each hosted model play Heartleaf when given the current best soul file?

The larger question

Inference is most of what a coworld game costs. We want to know which models play well, and which cheaper ones we could restrict players to without hurting their play.

This experiment

Hold the strategy constant and vary only the model. The soul file is the leaderboard's rank-1 Heartleaf policy, "tablekeeper v2". Each model gets the same prompt, the same seats, the same seeds, and the same 20-second clock.

Caveats6 / 7

What this does not tell us

One soul, one coworld

Every model played the same personality in the same game. A model that is mediocre with this prompt may be excellent with another, and Heartleaf is one social game, not a proxy for all coworlds.

Rounds 8 and 9 were broken

Our shared OpenRouter keys hit their spend caps mid-round, and the sidecar reported the 403s as generic internal errors. Round 8 lost 26% of all calls and round 9 lost 14%. Mean score fell from 103 per model-game in round 7 to 31. Opus 4.6, 4.8, 5, Sonnet 4.6, and Fable 5 were tested only in those rounds, so their numbers are not evidence about them. GPT-5.5 topped the table despite playing only in round 9.

A tournament, not a round-robin

Survivors played up to 35 games, newcomers 5, against different opponents each round. Nine-player scores are coupled. Five games is a small sample and per-model variance was large.

Route, not just model

Latency and availability measure the OpenRouter route we hit, under a strict 20-second clock. A "free" or preview route timing out says little about the underlying weights served properly.

The prompt argues with itself

The soul tells gnomes the "connection score" wins, but the evaluator scores dinners. Models that dutifully optimized conversation were following instructions, not being dumb.

Money

Five transport failures are unpriced, so the $1,222 total is reconciled but not a certified invoice. Costs are realized bills, and cache behavior meant list price did not predict them well.

Wrap-up7 / 7

What we take away

A handful of open-weight models play nearly as well as the frontier models at a hundredth of the price.

  • GLM 5.3 Flash, MiMo v2.5 Pro, and DeepSeek V4 Flash scored 90–126 points per game across 20–35 games each, for a fifth of a cent per point.
  • GPT-5.5, Kimi K3, and Gemini 3.8 Flash were the top raw scorers and still cheap in absolute terms, at one to two cents per point.
  • The Anthropic models scored respectably when the service was healthy but cost 10–60× more per point, so the elimination rule removed them early.

Some open routes are unusable, not merely weak.

  • Hy4 preview and MiniMax M3 free scored zero across five games: one never beat the clock, the other was never served.
  • Nemotron 550B, Qwen 3.8 Max, and Grok 4.6 have a median response slower than the 20-second deadline. Their transcripts show gnomes narrating that they missed dinner while waiting outside.
  • Reliability was the stronger filter than intelligence. Fast-but-wrong models lost turns to illegal actions; slow models lost them to the clock.

Next: re-run rounds 8–9 with fixed key capacity, then test the promising cheap models across several souls and a second coworld.
